# M4 — Budget bursts over ready beads

> **Rewritten 2026-07-25.** This replaces `2026-07-25-m4-backlog-bursts.md`, which was written
> before M4a shipped and assumed the scheduler owned a pool of tasks (`backlog_tasks`). It does
> not. Projects declare their work in **beads**, the scheduler polls `bd ready --json` per
> registered repo and mirrors nothing. What survived that change is the part that mattered —
> **the measured live ceiling** — and it survived unaltered. What died was the task pool, its
> CRUD, its tab, and the reaper.
>
> Read [`2026-07-25-m4a-beads-task-sources.md`](./2026-07-25-m4a-beads-task-sources.md) first;
> this document assumes its vocabulary (project states, `autoLabel`, leases, the completion
> marker, `handBack`).

Design: [`2026-07-25-launchbox-design.md`](../specs/2026-07-25-launchbox-design.md).
Depends on **M1** (usage + `run_usage` calibration), **M2** (guard, `admit`, the burn-down
planner this mirrors), **M3** (stop semantics, `stopped` status) and **M4a** (projects, leases,
the beads adapter, the poller). Verify: `npm test` plus a live drive.

**Goal:** spend a chosen slice of already-paid-for capacity working through ready beads —
*"work through my backlog using ~15% of my session limit"* — spread over time, and stop on
**measured** spend rather than on an estimate.

**Definition of done:** a burst previews which projects it will draw from, how many runs the
budget is expected to buy and with what confidence; on confirm it works through ready beads
spread over the window; and it **stops itself** when the measured delta reaches the budget,
however wrong the estimate was. Every bead it runs goes through the same lease → re-read →
claim → launch → marker → close path the poller uses. Nothing about a burst weakens M4a's
gates.

---

## 4.1 Why the measured ceiling is the core of this feature

*(Unchanged from the original plan. M4a did not touch it.)*

The API gives **percent, not tokens** (`limit_dollars` is `null` on this plan), so cost per task
can only be *learned*, and early estimates will be bad. A burst that trusted its estimate would
overspend.

So a burst carries two independent limits:

1. **Planning estimate** — chooses how much work to attempt and how to spread it, from
   `run_usage` medians. Best-effort, shown with its confidence.
2. **Measured live ceiling** — the burst records `startPct` for its window at kickoff; once
   `currentPct - startPct >= budgetPct`, every remaining attempt in that burst is refused.
   This is ground truth from M1 and it is what actually enforces the budget.

The estimate can be wildly wrong and the burst still respects the budget. That relationship is
the whole design; everything else is plumbing.

**Posture, from the design spec (§ "Design rule derived from this", launchbox-design.md:106):**
frame and default every budget feature as *"don't waste capacity already paid for,"* with
explicit user-set ceilings — **not** *"saturate the cap."* This binds the wording of the presets
and the meter (§4.8), and it is why defaults ship conservative and maximisation is opt-in. Also
binding: percent only, never a token or dollar figure (spec:153), and no feature may attempt to
defeat or extend a limit (spec:155).

## 4.2 The two rules M4a imposes on this milestone

These are not preferences. Each one is a bug M4a already paid for.

**Rule 1 — a burst must not claim ahead.** Claiming a bead at *plan* time and running it later
is the shipped bug M4a fixed: a claim makes the bead `in_progress`, which `bd ready` excludes,
so any path that gives up after claiming makes the bead invisible to every future poll —
*"retry later" meaning never*. It is also measurably rude: reads and `--claim` append **0** lines
to the git-tracked `.beads/interactions.jsonl`, but a hand-back appends **2**. A 20-slot burst
that claimed up front and tripped its ceiling at slot 3 would write ~34 uncommitted audit lines
into the user's repo for work that never ran. **A burst plans from `bd ready` (free) and claims
exactly one bead immediately before launching it.**

**Rule 2 — a burst must not materialise `once` entries.** The original §4.3 said each selected
task becomes a `jobs` row with a `once` entry, which "buys the M2 guard and M3 pause for free".
That is now false and actively dangerous:

- A bead's job row already exists and ships **deliberately disabled with a spent `once` entry**
  (`lib/projects.js:616-620`) precisely so the cron scheduler can never arm it.
- A scheduler-fired bead run bypasses everything M4a built: `acquireLease`, the pre-launch
  `bd show` re-read, `--claim`, `handBack`, and the `onDone` marker check. It could not close
  its bead and could not hand it back.
- It would **race the poller** for the same bead. The lease is the only thing preventing a
  double-run, and the cron path never takes it.
- The slots would be silently erased anyway: the next `pollProject` calls
  `updateJob(db, existing.id, shape)` with `enabled: false` and a spent schedule.
- "M3 pause for free" is false off the scheduler path — `hold` deliberately admits everything
  reaching the runner, which is why the poller calls `pause.blocksSchedule()` itself and
  re-checks it **between beads**.

**So the burst owns *timing and admission*; `lib/projects.js` owns *bead mechanics*.**

## 4.3 Execution: a burst drives the poller, and pins times but never bead identities

A burst is a **time-boxed, ceiling-enforced acceleration of the beads poller over a chosen set
of projects.** It holds a timetable of *attempt times*. At each attempt it asks the existing
poller to run the next eligible bead.

**Slots carry times, not bead ids.** This is a deliberate reversal of the original plan's
`slots: [{taskId, …}]`. Pinning bead X to 14:32 at plan time re-creates the mirror M4a rejected:
by 14:32 a human may have closed X, a dependency may have re-blocked it, or someone may have
claimed it. Beads owns readiness, so readiness is resolved **at launch**, by the same
`bd ready` → lease → `bd show` → claim sequence the poller already runs. Times are ours to
pin; identity is not.

Mechanically, `pollProject` already does everything an attempt needs — ready read, pause
re-check between beads, lease, re-read, claim, launch, `onDone`, hand-back on any non-`ok`
outcome, and stop-after-first-guard-refusal. An attempt is therefore a **poll of one project
with a burst context attached**, not a new execution path. `lib/burst.js` adds the timetable,
the ceiling, and the project selection; it adds no way to launch a bead.

Consequences:

- The `minGapMin` spread survives and matters more than before: it stops a burst spending 15%
  of the window in three minutes, and it keeps attempts far enough apart that the usage
  snapshot driving the ceiling has refreshed between them (poll floor 60s, default 180s).
- A burst attempt that finds nothing ready is a no-op that consumes a slot and says so. It must
  not be an error: beads may legitimately have run dry.
- Because identity is late-bound, **the preview is explicitly advisory about *which* work runs**
  and precise only about the budget. The UI must say that rather than implying a fixed list.

## 4.4 Data model

```sql
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS bursts (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  window TEXT NOT NULL,                  -- five_hour | seven_day | …
  budgetPct REAL NOT NULL,
  startPct REAL, currentPct REAL,
  projectIds TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT '[]', -- JSON array; the burst draws only from these
  slots TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT '[]',      -- JSON array of ISO attempt times, consumed in order
  maxRuns INTEGER, minGapMin INTEGER,
  runs INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,       -- attempts that actually launched
  state TEXT NOT NULL,                   -- planned | active | spent | done | cancelled
  reason TEXT,                           -- why it left `active`, in one human sentence
  startedAt TEXT, finishedAt TEXT, createdAt TEXT NOT NULL
);
```

`backlog_tasks` is **not built** — it is the mirror M4a rejected. `task_leases` is already keyed
`(projectId, beadId)`, which is the right granularity, and `run_usage` already accumulates real
per-bead cost because one job row per bead is reused across runs (M4a §4a.7), so there is no
new cost table either.

Notes:

- `bursts` is a pure addition — `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` in `SCHEMA` is enough, no
  `migrate()` `ALTER` needed (that dance is only for a new *column* on a shipped table; the
  `readyCount` precedent is at `lib/db.js:121-127`).
- Must be wiped by `cleanupAll()` (`lib/db.js:536`) — the original plan said "both tables";
  it is now one.
- `reason` exists because a burst that stopped silently is indistinguishable from one that
  finished. Every exit from `active` writes a sentence.
- **At most one `active` burst.** Two concurrent bursts against the same window would each
  measure the other's spend as its own. Enforced on insert.

## 4.5 Per-bead worktrees (folded in from the M4a review)

Today effective concurrency is **clamped to 1 per project** whenever a worktree is in use,
because there is one worktree per project: two of our own beads would share a checkout *and a
branch* and edit each other's files. That clamp is correct, and it is also the ceiling on how
fast a burst can work — so it is lifted here, where there is finally a reason to.

- `worktreeName(project)` / `branchFor(project)` (`lib/worktree.js:33,40`) become bead-aware:
  path `<root>/<proj>-<projId8>/<sanitised beadId>`, branch `scheduler/<proj>/<beadId>`. Both
  `ensure` and `remove` recompute the path independently, so both take the new argument or the
  reap targets the wrong directory. Bead ids are short and prefix-collide (`sp-1` vs `sp-12` —
  the exact bug the anchored marker check fixed), so sanitising must disambiguate, not truncate.
- Reuse-the-branch-on-retry stays: one bead keeps its branch across attempts, mirroring
  one-job-row-per-bead.
- **Reaping becomes mandatory.** `worktree.remove` has **no production caller today** — nothing
  reaps. Per-bead worktrees without reaping is an unbounded disk leak. Reap on lease release:
  every `releaseLease`/`completeLease` site in `onDone` gets a paired best-effort `remove`, and
  `recoverOrphans` sweeps worktrees a crash left behind (`git worktree list` plus the lease
  table identifies them).
- The clamp at `lib/projects.js:439-451` and its explanatory `reasons.push` then go, and
  `cap` reverts to `config.maxConcurrent`. `tests/projects.test.js:1001` asserts the current
  behaviour and must be inverted rather than deleted.
- ⚠️ Per-bead worktrees multiply checkout cost. `maxConcurrent` stays defaulted to **1** — this
  lifts a hazard-driven clamp, it does not make parallelism the default.

## 4.6 Planning & preview

`lib/burst.js`: `createBurst({ db, usage, budget, projects, beads, now })` →
`{ plan, start, cancel, status, admit, tick }`

`plan({ window, budgetPct, projectIds, maxRuns, minGapMin, deadline })` → preview:

```js
{ ok: true, window, budgetPct,
  slots: [ISO, …],                    // attempt times only — identity is resolved at launch
  ready: [{projectId, name, readyCount}],
  estimate: {perRunPct, expectedRuns, source: 'learned'|'assumed', samples},
  usablePct, reserveCapPct, headroomPct,
  confidence: 'high'|'low', assumptions: [string] }
```

or `{ ok: false, reason: '<one human sentence>' }` — the M2 shape, so the UI's existing error
precedence (`reason` → `errors[]` → `error`) works unchanged.

- **Do not write a second planner.** `lib/budget.js:133 plan()` already does reserve-capped,
  never-past-reset, min-gap, `avgDeltaForJob`-costed slotting with confidence and a plain-language
  `assumptions[]`. A burst's timetable is that arithmetic with a different candidate set, so it
  extends or reuses it. Two implementations of the same budget maths can disagree, which is
  exactly what `lib/budget.js:40` warns against.
- **Capped by the same reserve `admit()` enforces** — `reserveFiveHourPct` / `reserveWeeklyPct`.
  If `budgetPct` exceeds headroom under the reserve, the preview **says so and offers the
  reduced figure** rather than silently trimming.
- Slots start at `now + PLAN_LEAD_MS` (5 min, `lib/budget.js:35`) so the plan cannot expire
  while it is on screen, honour `minGapMin`, and **never pass `resetsAt`** for the window.
- **Cost per run** comes from `avgDeltaForJob` medians over the candidate projects' bead job
  rows, not from a per-task override (there is no store for one, and inventing one would be a
  mirror). Fall back to `ASSUMED_COST_PCT`, and mark `confidence: 'low'` when
  `samples < MIN_SAMPLES`. A per-bead estimate, if ever wanted, belongs in the repo's own
  `.scheduler.json`.
- **Candidate projects must be `active`.** `GET /api/projects/:id/ready` deliberately works on a
  `pending` project so a human can see what *would* run before activating — so a planner that
  naively read ready-work would plan, and then run, work from a repo nobody activated. That is
  the activation airlock, defeated. Filter on `state === 'active'`, and skip `paused`.
- Selection within a project stays beads' business: `bd ready` is already priority-sorted
  (int 0-4, `0` = critical), and the `autoLabel` filter is applied by the adapter with
  `labels ?? []`.
- **Always a preview the user confirms.** No burst starts implicitly. `plan()` writes nothing.
- The preview must state the live posture honestly: with shipped defaults this account's guard
  stands the scheduler fully down (a weekly bucket at 90% `critical` + active blocks *every*
  scheduled fire under `pauseOnWarning`). A burst planned into that must say **that**, in those
  words, rather than rendering an empty plan.

## 4.7 Enforcement

`start()` records `startPct` from the same `usage.snapshot()` the guard reads — never a second
source — and sets `state: 'active'`.

`tick()` fires on the next due slot and, in order:

1. `state !== 'active'` → stop.
2. `pause.blocksSchedule()` → skip this slot with a reason, keep the burst active. A pause stops
   launching, not the burst.
3. Snapshot **untrustworthy** (missing, `available: false`, or older than `2 × pollSec`) →
   **hold the slot; do not spend.** This is the milestone's one deliberate departure from the
   design spec's fail-open rule (spec:56): the general guard fails open so a monitoring break
   cannot stop the scheduler, but a burst without measurement **has no ceiling at all**, so it
   fails **closed**. Say so in the UI, and keep saying it.
4. `currentPct - startPct >= budgetPct` → `state: 'spent'`, write `reason`, notify **once**.
5. `runs >= maxRuns` → `done`.
6. Otherwise attempt one bead via the poller path, and increment `runs` only if one launched.

**Window reset mid-burst ends the burst.** If `currentPct` drops below `startPct`, the window it
was budgeted against no longer exists; a budget is scoped to one window instance. End it with a
reason rather than re-baselining — re-baselining silently doubles the spend the user approved.

**When a burst leaves `active`, drop its remaining slots** — there are no materialised jobs to
disable (Rule 2), so there is nothing to sweep. And **any bead already claimed must be handed
back** through `handBack`, never merely released: a released lease on an `in_progress` bead is
bug #1 verbatim. Only claims we placed (`assignee === BD_ACTOR`).

**Trigger naming.** The poller launches with `runner.start(job, 'beads')`. A burst attempt needs
its own trigger so it is legible in history and so admission treats it correctly — note
`budget.admit` allows `trigger === 'manual'` unconditionally, so a burst must **not** borrow it
or every burst run would skip the guard. A burst run is subject to the normal guard *and* the
burst ceiling.

**Cancel** stops future slots; in-flight runs follow M3 stop semantics, and a `stopped` run is
not a task failure — the bead is handed back, not closed.

## 4.8 API & UI

- `POST /api/bursts/plan` → preview, **writes nothing** (200, or 400 carrying `reason`)
- `POST /api/bursts` → start from a confirmed plan (re-validates rather than trusting the client)
- `GET /api/bursts/active`, `GET /api/bursts/:id`
- `POST /api/bursts/:id/cancel`

Follow `/api/budget/plan` + `/api/budget/plan/apply` (`server.js:337-384`) closely: confirm
re-derives instead of trusting the preview, rejects an expired first slot **wholesale** rather
than partially applying, and the client posts the server's slots back **verbatim** with no
client-side arithmetic that could disagree.

UI — **no new tab.** The Projects tab shipped in M4a is where ready work lives, so the burst
surface goes there:

- A **Burst** button with presets — `10% of session` · `25% of session` · `5% of weekly` ·
  custom — plus max-runs, min-gap, and project checkboxes (defaulting to all `active`).
- The preview is the important screen: per-project ready counts, expected runs and the
  per-run cost with its sample count, the headroom and the reserve cap, the confidence badge,
  and the assumptions in plain language ("no bead in these projects has run yet; cost is a
  guess"). It must state that the *set* of beads is resolved at launch. Confirm/Cancel.
- Active-burst strip: a spent-vs-budget meter driven by **measured** percent, runs done,
  slots remaining, next attempt, and Cancel. Worded as spending a chosen slice, per §4.1's
  posture rule — never as filling the cap.
- Settings: `burstMinGapMin`. `backlogReapDays` is **not** built (§4.10) and
  `backlogDefaultEstPct` duplicates `ASSUMED_COST_PCT`.

## 4.9 Tests

- `plan()` writes nothing; refuses with a sentence when the window has no `percent`/`resetsAt`
- plan: capped by reserve; offers the reduced figure; never slots past `resetsAt`; honours
  `minGapMin`; first slot respects `PLAN_LEAD_MS`
- plan excludes `pending` and `paused` projects — the airlock, asserted directly
- estimate sourcing: learned median → assumed default, with the right `source`/`confidence`
- enforcement: once measured delta ≥ budget the burst flips to `spent`, further slots are
  refused, and the reason is recorded
- **stale/unavailable usage holds a slot and spends nothing** — the deliberate fail-*closed*
  exception, asserted as such
- window reset mid-burst ends the burst rather than re-baselining
- a burst attempt goes through the lease: a bead already leased is not double-run
- a burst-claimed bead is **handed back** (`--status open --assignee ""`), not merely released,
  when the ceiling trips, on cancel, and on a `fail`/`stopped` run
- a burst run that exits `ok` **without** the completion marker hands the bead back
- pause landing between slots stops launching without ending the burst; `hold` is honoured
- at most one `active` burst
- per-bead worktrees: two beads from one project get distinct paths and branches; prefix-colliding
  ids do not collide; the worktree is reaped on lease release; `recoverOrphans` sweeps a crashed
  run's worktree
- `cleanupAll` wipes `bursts`
- regression: a bead's job row cannot be armed by the burn-down planner (§4.10)

## 4.10 Two pre-existing defects to fix here

Both were found reviewing this plan against shipped code. Neither is caused by M4, and both sit
directly in its blast radius.

1. **The M2 burn-down planner can arm a bead's job row.** `openPlanDialog()`
   (`public/app.js:514-516`) lists **every** job, bead rows included, merely labelled
   "(disabled)" — and `/api/budget/plan/apply` force-enables whatever is picked, by design
   ("a disabled job would swallow every slot silently, so enable it"). Confirmed in the shipped
   code. The result is a bead run fired by the cron scheduler with **no lease, no claim, no
   marker check and no close** — able to race the poller for the same bead, which is the one
   thing the lease exists to prevent.
   **FIXED 2026-07-25, and reproducing it live showed it was worse than written here.** The
   first draft of this section said the next poll would rewrite the row to `enabled: false`, so
   the damage was "intermittent rather than permanent". Wrong: `apply()` round-trips the row
   through `validateJob`, and `validateFields` drops keys the extension does not declare — so
   **`_beadId` and `_projectId` are stripped**. Measured: the row came back `enabled: true` with
   a real future `nextFire`, no `_beadId`, and `model` silently rewritten from the project's
   configured value to `default`. That **detaches the row from its bead permanently**:
   `findJobByBead` stops matching, so the poller can neither heal the row nor reuse it — it
   mints a *second* row for the same bead, discarding the accumulated cost history §4a.7 depends
   on, while the orphan keeps firing the bead's prompt on a schedule with no lease, claim or
   close. Nothing self-heals. Fixed by excluding rows carrying `params._beadId` from the
   planner's candidates in `/api/budget/plan`, in `/api/budget/plan/apply` (checked
   independently, since apply is the call that enables), and in the dialog, which now says how
   many bead tasks it hid and why.
2. **`config.budget` from a repo's `.scheduler.json` is never validated.** `parseProjectConfig`
   accepts any object (`lib/projects.js:104`) and `materialise` copies it straight onto
   `params.budget` (`:611`), bypassing `budgetParams` (`lib/validate.js:111`). So a repo can
   declare `{"budget":{"ignoreGuard":true}}` — or a typo shaped like it — and unattended runs
   skip the budget guard entirely, with no validation and no error. Route it through
   `budgetParams`, and treat an unrecognised value as an error, exactly as `permMode` does.

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Estimates are bad early, so a burst overspends | The measured ceiling (§4.1) is the real enforcement; the estimate only sizes the timetable. |
| Fail-open would remove the ceiling entirely | Bursts fail **closed** — no measurement, no spending. The documented single exception to spec:56. |
| Reset mid-burst makes the budget meaningless | End the burst; a budget is scoped to one window instance. |
| A burst claims beads it never runs, dirtying the user's repo | Rule 1 — claim one bead immediately before launching it; plan from free reads. |
| A burst bypasses the lease/marker/close path | Rule 2 — the burst never launches; `lib/projects.js` does. Asserted by test. |
| A burst runs work from a project nobody activated | Candidates filtered to `state === 'active'`; asserted directly, because `/ready` deliberately works on `pending`. |
| `hold` leaves a burst running | `pause.blocksSchedule()` checked per slot, as the poller does per bead. |
| Per-bead worktrees leak disk | Reaping is mandatory and tied to lease release, plus an orphan sweep. `maxConcurrent` stays 1 by default. |
| Two bursts measure each other's spend | At most one `active` burst, enforced on insert. |
